Why can the sampling intervals overlap while an improvement interval excludes zero?

The sampling chart asks how uncertain each overall rate is. The intervention chart asks how uncertain the difference is when both methods are evaluated on the same questions. A difficult question often raises both methods’ errors; that shared variation partly cancels when we compare them. Overlapping overall-rate intervals are therefore not a test of the paired difference.

For Hallucination (chat), the original lens is 47.63% (95% interval 44.20–51.09%) and best-of-32 is 45.82% (42.28–49.37%). The paired reduction is 1.80 percentage points (0.42–3.23). That individual exploratory comparison excludes zero. Separately, the interval adjusted for the original many-comparison menu, against the four-bullet baseline, includes zero. These answer different questions and use explicitly different references.

What counts as success?

Hallucination (chat)
Fraction of specific readouts contradicted by the transcript. Lower is better. This counts readouts, not questions.

Multihop (multi-token)
Questions with all required intermediate entities recovered together in one readout. The final answer is optional. Higher is better.

Chained intermediates
Questions with a required intermediate number ranked first by the judge at a reading site. Higher is better.

Role-bound association
Questions with the entity–role bindings recovered at a reading site. Higher is better.

Reading the evidence

Paired comparisons matter.
The same questions and related groups are compared across methods. Overlapping per-method bars are not a test of the difference.

More methods do not mean more questions.
There are 149, 94, 42 and 50 related groups across the four tasks. Simultaneous uncertainty is reported separately when the declared comparison family is complete.

Quality and effort are both visible.
Sample count, helper calls, new scoring and actual output length accompany the final results. Shortening may remove errors and useful content together.
