## Decision and limits

Root and two independent scientific reviewers, `adversarial_evaluation` and
`model_scoring_audit`, support prospective A16 and unpruned Emean32 comparisons.
They do not establish a globally faithful explanation method.

Against capped N1, A16's validation cell differences are hallucination error
−0.84 percentage points, MT recovery +2.27, numerical recovery +0.91 and role
recovery +0.53. Its candidate budget is half N32's. Emean32 has the lowest
observed unpruned hallucination error, 38.66% versus 41.13% for capped N1, while
preserving similar numerical recovery. Against A32, Emean32 recovers four more
numerical cells, +1.82 points, with similar output length.

Most paired intervals remain broad. Validation has twenty items per family and
only seven numerical and ten role groups. Multiple exploratory comparisons are
unadjusted, and significance is not an advancement gate. Hall off-topic outputs
rise from 7.88% for capped N1 to 8.91% for Emean32. Report that alongside error.

Recovered names or intermediate numbers often appear among conflicting entities
or incorrect arithmetic. Some role verdicts depend on inconsistent matching of
names and descriptions; an A16 burglar/neighbor "win" even retains a reversed
relation in the judge rationale. These remain official verdicts with preserved
evidence, not corrected scores. Systematic wins, regressions and seeded unchanged
traces explain limitations rather than prove general faithfulness.

Pruning roughly halves output length and loses MT/numerical recovery. Matched
random deletion matches or improves its contradiction rate and often retains
more numerical information. Pooling increases search work and output length
without a balanced validation gain. Neither is promoted; their completed
validation results remain valuable negative or inconclusive evidence.
